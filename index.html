Spectrometer Designer

This calculator is used to estimate the critical optical parameters of a spectrometer based on its grating and focal length. It helps optical engineers determine the linear dispersion at the detector and the overall resolving power of the system.

Mathematical Formulas

dx = f × (dβ/dλ) × Δλ
R = W × (dβ/dλ)

Variable Definitions

dx Linear dispersion at the focal plane (mm)
f Focal length of the collimator/focusing mirror (mm)
dβ/dλ Angular dispersion of the grating (rad/nm)
Δλ Wavelength interval being resolved (nm)
R Resolving power (dimensionless)
W Illuminated grating width (nm)
⚠️
Note on Units: For resolving power (R) to be dimensionless, the illuminated width W must be expressed in the same unit as the wavelength (nanometers).

Raman Waveshift Converter

Raman spectroscopy relies on the inelastic scattering of light. This calculator converts between the wavelength of scattered light (in nanometers) and the Raman shift (in wavenumbers). Positive shifts indicate Stokes scattering, while negative shifts indicate anti-Stokes scattering.

Mathematical Formulas

Δν̃ = 107 × (1/λ0 − 1/λR)
λR = 1 / (1/λ0 − Δν̃/107)

Variable Definitions

Δν̃ Raman shift (cm-1)
λ0 Excitation laser wavelength (nm)
λR Scattered light wavelength (nm)
⚠️
Note on Scattering: A scattered wavelength greater than the excitation wavelength yields a positive (Stokes) shift. A wavelength smaller than the excitation yields a negative (anti-Stokes) shift.

Grating Order Calculator

Diffraction gratings disperse light into multiple orders based on the grating equation. This calculator determines the diffraction angle for a specific order and wavelength, and verifies if the order is physically propagating.

Mathematical Formulas

β = arcsin(mλ/d − sin α)
d (nm) = 106 / groove density (lines/mm)

Variable Definitions

β Angle of diffraction (degrees)
α Angle of incidence (degrees)
m Diffraction order (integer)
λ Light wavelength (nm)
d Grating period (nm)
⚠️
Non-Propagating Orders: If the argument of the arcsine function is outside the range of -1 to +1, the diffraction order does not physically propagate and is rejected by the calculator.

Pulse Compression Calculator

In ultrafast optics, grating pairs are used to compress chirped laser pulses. This calculator determines the Group Delay Dispersion (GDD) introduced by a grating pair in a double-pass configuration.

Mathematical Formulas

Φ″ = −[4Lλ3 / (2πc2d2)] × [1 − (λ/d − sin α)2]−3/2

Variable Definitions

Φ″ Group Delay Dispersion (fs2)
L Perpendicular separation between gratings (mm)
λ Center wavelength (nm)
c Speed of light (m/s)
d Grating period (nm)
α Angle of incidence (degrees)
⚠️
Note on TOD: The calculator also provides an approximation for the Third-Order Dispersion (TOD) based on the computed GDD.

Pulse Front Tilt Calculator

When a pulse passes through a diffraction grating, the pulse front becomes tilted relative to the phase front. This calculator determines the required incidence and diffraction angles to achieve a specific pulse front tilt angle.

Mathematical Formulas

γ = arctan[mλ / (d × cos β)]
sin β = mλ/d − sin α

Variable Definitions

γ Pulse front tilt angle (degrees)
α Angle of incidence (degrees)
β Angle of diffraction (degrees)
m Diffraction order
λ Light wavelength (nm)
d Grating period (nm)
